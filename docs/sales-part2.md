# Sales Part 2 backend

## Versioned OpenRouter pipeline

New sessions can opt into the Sales-only OpenRouter pipeline, currently v3 (see the section on customer concerns below). Jev recognizes independent player acts, backend evidence rules own progression and assessment, Qwen writes Lan's response from an approved plan, and local Supertonic speaks committed text. The rollout default remains legacy. Shadow mode observes Jev without changing legacy gameplay.

V2 allows at most eight processed dialogue turns by default and requires a player response after the trust challenge for restored trust. Completing the objectives ends the conversation immediately, including when turns remain. The limit is strict: a solution completed on the last turn does not grant a ninth turn or emit a challenge the player cannot answer. `supplementalTurnGranted` remains false for new sessions. An explicit manager transfer, maintained unauthorized promise or two valid silent turns can also end it early. A client completion request after an accepted turn records `stopped_early` when the objectives are incomplete.

OpenRouter turns expose `playerResponseRating` as `good`, `bad`, `neutral` or `uncertain`. A semantic uncertainty returns `uncertain`; null means no usable classification result, such as a classifier service failure or entirely malformed/missing evidence. A Luna service error still returns `uncertain` when Jev supplied valid but ambiguous probabilities. Uncertain turns preserve their unresolved status and do not increment the evaluable-turn count. Every successfully classified utterance, including an uncertain one, consumes one dialogue turn; technical failures and identical retries do not. An uncertain label never creates a penalty; separately certain violations or bad conduct preserve a `bad` rating even when another label is uncertain. `assessmentUncertain` controls whether the turn can be assessed, independently of its rating and the dialogue budget. A certain ending can stop the dialogue while unresolved critical evidence holds the final score pending or for review. The rubric credits each contextual skill once and keeps violation events separate from outstanding promises. Only good/bad ratings increment the corresponding response counts; historical records keep their stored values.

Writer prompts `lan-writer-v2.2` and `lan-writer-v2.3` disable Qwen reasoning for the short speech request. Reasoning previously consumed all 220 output tokens and returned empty `content` with `finish_reason=length`. The adapter keeps safe response-shape metadata (`finishReason`, `contentChars`, `reasoningTokens`), guard rejection codes and timeout diagnostics without retaining reasoning text. The pipeline watchdog allows 250 ms after the adapter deadline to return these diagnostics. Unexpected writer failures also carry a safe error code.

The reply planner acknowledges an exchange already offered during investigation and asks about the missing walking, fit or cause context. It does not treat unfinished objectives as unclear speech. Known insults produce a firm warning even when a different label is uncertain. Required-content guards prevent Qwen from replacing these plans with generic repetition requests. Insults alone still warn rather than automatically ending the dialogue.

These fixes apply to new sessions after restarting the backend. Existing completed results retain their frozen assessment. Active sessions using the previous rubric/prompt cannot resume under the new versions; start a new simulation run.

The initial validation on 2026-10-04 covered 70 backend adapter/API cases and 16 Web result-contract cases. The earlier 11-turn recorded session was replayed without changing the recording. Three live Qwen probes using its exchange, warning and ending plans returned validated speech without fallback in 0.74–1.41 seconds, with zero reasoning tokens. Backend checks cover early success at six turns, explicit manager termination after one turn, success at the eighth turn, and a last-turn solution without an extra turn. No headset retest or APK build was performed for these backend/Web changes.

A subsequent four-turn run exposed a separate phase-1 loop: all four writer calls succeeded with `fallbackUsed=false`, but copied the same default plan. Accepted apologies and exchanges did not complete objective 1 because no open complaint question was asked; the planner then supplied no follow-up question. Prompt `lan-writer-v2.3` and the planner now acknowledge these acts and invite the missing complaint interaction without changing assessment evidence. Questions asked on the utterance that completes objective 1 can also disclose their answers after entering objective 2. Emotional/investigation follow-ups reject an exact repeat of the previous reply, and safe fallbacks select a different wording of the same required question. Each writer attempt records its rejection codes, including successful rewrites. Focused coverage increased to 76 backend cases; all four new-run plans replayed successfully and three live Qwen checks returned validated, non-repeated replies without fallback in 0.78–2.20 seconds. One used the bounded rewrite.

Sessions freeze all assessment versions, reuse successful checkpoints for retry, and preserve backend terminal authority in the simulation aggregate. Historical sessions retain their old rules and final scorer. The four-turn behavior and classifier keyword grounding described in the legacy sections below do not apply to v2. See [rollout and verification](verification/sales-openrouter-v2.md) and [the assessment decision](adr/0003-versioned-sales-evidence-assessment.md).

### Continue dialogue while assessment needs review

The follow-up correction uses rubric `sales-rubric-v2.3` and prompt `lan-writer-v2.5`. A review flag alone no longer ends dialogue or submits completion. Unity continues normal replies and recording while turns remain, including after resuming an active review session. Completed objectives and the existing explicit endings can still finish the conversation early. Review UI appears only after dialogue ends.

`dialogueTurnCount` counts committed classified utterances, including uncertain turns, and owns `remainingTurns`. `evaluableTurnCount` and `acceptedTurnCount` still count turns with assessable evidence. Thus repeated classifier uncertainty cannot extend an eight-turn conversation indefinitely. Cached turn retries preserve both counters, and technical failures do not consume a dialogue turn. A completion request closes dialogue even when no turn was evaluable and the assessment needs review; it persists `awaitingCompletion`, the ending reason and the review status without inventing a score. Subsequent new turns are rejected, while previously committed turn retries remain idempotent. A review ending uses neutral closure without claiming restored or lost trust. Final assessment remains pending review.

## Legacy pipeline

The `sale` scene links Part 1 and Lan's returning-customer conversation with a pseudonymous `sessionId`. The backend stores the authoritative conversation, phase, accepted-turn count, silence count, and final evaluation. Customer replies and final analysis use separate constrained LLM requests.

The final analysis scores two criteria from 0 to 50 each. `apologyAndPolicyRemedy` measures whether the player apologizes or acknowledges the disappointment and offers a complete remedy allowed by store policy. `adaptabilityAndDeescalation` measures relevant questioning, adaptation to the customer's answers, calm objection handling, and de-escalation. Their sum is stored as `rawScore`. Each recorded store-policy violation deducts 10 points, down to zero, and the final value is stored as `score`. The result also stores `policyViolationPenalty` and the violation list.

- More `good` turns than `bad` turns: `customerRating: good` and `trustState: restored`.
- More `bad` turns than `good` turns: `customerRating: bad` and `trustState: lost`.
- Equal counts: `customerRating: considering` and `trustState: partially_restored`.

The terminal customer line follows the same counts. A good majority uses one of the return-intent lines, a bad majority uses one of the rejection or manager-escalation lines, and a tie uses the consideration line. Two silences without a rated turn remain a lost-trust outcome.

For each turn, the model returns semantic evidence flags, a bounded `replyIntent`, and disclosed fact IDs for the player's transcript. The backend derives the final `good` or `bad` rating from those flags. A polite response is good when it includes apology plus remedy, explanation plus corrective advice, a reasonable policy-compliant return policy, or a relevant question about the customer's pain, use, or fit. A concrete remedy can also count as good when the player apologized in an earlier accepted turn. Rude or condescending speech, profanity, apology without a solution, asking the customer to keep trying the product without an explanation, and repeating a question already answered are bad. The session stores every accepted player transcript. The model receives all prior player transcripts, Lan's previous lines, and the current dialogue state so it can use earlier answers and detect repeated questions. It never supplies customer prose. The backend checks the intent against explicit transcript evidence and picks Lan's reply from fixed variants adapted from `NEW SALE PROMPT.md`, excluding lines already spoken in the session. When a player has already offered an exchange plan, a generic bad-intent reply cannot ask for that plan again; Lan asks about the unresolved complaint or acknowledges the plan. A direct question about pain location has its own reply pool. The backend also derives `activeObjective`, `objectiveCompleted`, `conversationComplete`, and `deterministicEnding` from the current session and fixed gameplay rules.

In objective 1, the backend remembers an earlier acknowledgment or apology in the same objective, so a later relevant open question can advance the conversation. Lan asks for the exchange policy only in the policy objective, and an explicitly stated seven-day exchange policy is recognized even if the model omits its flag. A demand for a receipt gets Lan's missing-receipt objection. A question about information Lan already stated, including information she volunteered without being asked, gets an explicit reminder that she has said it. Explicit insults and unauthorized discounts are checked against the transcript when the model misses them. The completed backend session owns the Part 2 score; Unity's terminal fragment cannot overwrite it with a default score. On disconnect or startup, a run with a completed session and all required data is finalized instead of marked aborted.

## Configuration

- `LLM_USE_AMD_HYBRID=false`: use the local llama.cpp service selected by `LLM_BASE_URL` and `LLM_MODEL`. Set the flag to `true` to use Lemonade's `Qwen3-4B-Hybrid` model instead. The hybrid branch accepts the model's compact JSON classifications and validates them before advancing game state. Gameplay quality still needs feedback from live sessions.
- `SHERPA_MODEL_DIR`, `SHERPA_NUM_THREADS`, and `SHERPA_TIMEOUT_SECONDS`: local sherpa-onnx Zipformer model location and bounded CPU inference settings. The controlled research sessions accept Vietnamese speech only and do not run automatic language identification.
- `RECORDINGS_DIR`: existing diagnostic storage directory.
- `SALES_RETENTION_DAYS`: default 30, configurable from 1 to 3650. Cleanup runs at startup, every hour, and on session-state reads. It removes expired Part 2 audio and transcripts, and linked Part 1 diagnostics. Tombstones prevent retries from recreating deleted data.
- `BACKEND_API_TOKEN`: optional existing gameplay bearer token. When enabled, provide it to the Unity request service at runtime through `BearerToken`; do not serialize credentials into scenes.
- `SUPERTONIC_BASE_URL`: loopback URL for the separately managed Supertonic sidecar. It defaults to `http://127.0.0.1:7788`.
- `TTS_TIMEOUT_SECONDS`: end-to-end speech deadline, including the one-request queue. It defaults to 8 seconds.
- `TTS_QUALITY`: Supertonic inference steps, from 1 to 20. It defaults to 5. Higher values sound cleaner but take longer, so raise `TTS_TIMEOUT_SECONDS` with it if synthesis starts timing out.
- `DISABLE_AI_SALE_PT2`: set to `true` to omit speech metadata and disable TTS routes. Sales responses continue to include customer text.
- `SALES_DIAGNOSTIC_TOKEN`: separate bearer token required for diagnostic deletion. Deletion is disabled when this value is absent.

Run one backend worker for this file-backed store. Atomic file replacement protects completed turns across process restarts; per-session locks serialize concurrent requests within the worker. Multiple workers sharing the directory require a transactional store or cross-process locking.

## HTTP contract

| Method and route | Request / behavior |
| --- | --- |
| `POST /api/sales/sessions` | `{ "sessionId": "...", "part1AttemptId": "..." }`; omit the optional Part 1 ID until uploaded. Creates or resumes without resetting progress. |
| `GET /api/sales/sessions/{sessionId}` | Current phase, counts, last customer text, and completion status; excludes stored conversation and turn caches. |
| `GET /api/sales/sessions/{sessionId}/speech/{speechId}` | Resolves an opaque speech ID to authoritative Lan text, synthesizes a complete WAV in memory, and returns `audio/wav`. It never accepts caller text. |
| `POST /api/tts` | Internal bounded text-to-speech endpoint. Accepts `{ "text": "..." }` with at most 600 characters and returns a complete WAV. |
| `POST /api/sales/sessions/{sessionId}/turns` | `turnId`, `clientVersion`, `retryCount`, and `audio` with `mimeType`, `encoding`, `sampleRateHz`, `channels`, `dataBase64`. Audio is mono PCM16 WAV at 16 kHz. |
| `POST /api/sales/sessions/{sessionId}/complete` | `completionId` and `reason` (`natural`, `time_limit`, or `turn_limit`). `natural` can finish an active session after at least one accepted turn. Returns criterion scores, `rawScore`, the policy-violation list and penalty, `score`, good/bad counts, `customerRating`, `trustState`, and `finalCustomerText`. Final states are immutable; retrying returns the existing result. |
| `DELETE /api/sales/sessions/{sessionId}/diagnostics` | Requires the diagnostic bearer token. Deletes Part 2 and linked Part 1 audio/transcripts and retains a deletion audit. |

Retry a failed turn with the same `turnId` and identical audio. A repeated accepted turn returns its cached response without incrementing counters. Changed audio under an existing ID is rejected. Silence does not consume an accepted turn. Infrastructure failures return a sanitized failure category, never a failed-performance result. Four accepted turns request final analysis. Lan can also end earlier when the player has acknowledged her disappointment and proposed a policy-compliant exchange to a lightweight walking shoe with a fit or walking trial. The final analysis reuses that terminal line. A client can request `natural` completion after one accepted turn. Profanity and insults receive a bad rating, a warning reply, and a recorded policy violation, but the conversation continues. Two valid silences, manager escalation, or a maintained unauthorized promise end with lost trust.

Unless `DISABLE_AI_SALE_PT2=true`, each opening, turn, and terminal customer line includes `speech` metadata with an opaque `speechId`, a sales speech URL, WAV format, and `available` state. The backend holds no generated audio after sending its response. It permits one synthesis and one queued request. A third request receives `429` with `tts_busy`; unavailable sidecars use `tts_unavailable`, the shared deadline uses `tts_timeout`, and invalid sidecar WAV data uses `tts_invalid_audio`. These details do not enter sales results or diagnostics.

Diagnostic records include audio, transcript, phase, retry count, format, processing duration, and available model metadata. The LLM server version is marked unavailable because its API does not provide a verified version. Raw diagnostics stay in the backend store; they must not be copied into general telemetry or logs.

## Microphone startup after the opening, 2026-10-05

A headset run reported `sales.part2.incomplete` several times right after `opening_shown`, before any turn. Unity opens the microphone only after the opening line, and a stalled or all-zero Quest start used to end the attempt after one startup cycle; a later manual retry succeeded. Stored headset WAVs always contain a nonzero noise floor, so all-zero startup is a device failure rather than player silence. Unity now retries up to three startup cycles (`microphoneStartupCycles`) before showing the retry screen. One Android interruption reported through both focus loss and pause emits one `incomplete`, and the permission dialog no longer counts as an interruption. Every `sales.part2.incomplete` carries `errorCode` (for example `microphone_zero_pcm` or `application_paused`), which the backend log prints. This requires a new APK.

## Customer concerns and ledger scoring (v3), 2026-10-05

New sessions use pipeline sales-openrouter-v3, rubric sales-rubric-v3 and prompt lan-writer-v3. Scenario, question set and thresholds are unchanged, so Jev labels need no recalibration. Sessions on earlier versions keep their results, and an open session on an earlier version cannot resume; restart the backend and start a new simulation. No APK change is needed: turn and completion responses keep their shape.

Lan reacts to four customer concerns instead of the active objective: not feeling heard, not understanding why the shoes hurt, having no suitable remedy, and fearing a repeat after the trust challenge. Each turn she reacts to bad conduct first (refusal, disrespect or abuse, pressure to keep wearing the shoes, an outstanding promise, or a clarification for uncertain labels), then answers fact questions, then raises the lowest-numbered open concern and its missing part. A fact question in a turn that is not bad is answered at once, at any point, up to two facts per reply; a bad turn reveals nothing. Lan says she already answered only when every fact the question targets was known before the turn; Jev's repeatedQuestion label is stored but not used.

When a turn with certain labels is not bad, earns no new component, reveals no new fact and leaves the raised concern open, that concern's hint level rises from 0 (general complaint) to 1 (specific complaint about the missing part) to 2 (Lan says directly what she needs, still as a customer). Bad and uncertain turns keep the level, and a level never falls until its concern is resolved. Another such turn at level 2 ends the conversation with endingReason stalemate; Lan closes according to the outcome's trustState and asks for a manager only if the session included bad conduct. Eight turns remains the upper limit.

One pure function in app/sales_concerns.py scores the whole evidence ledger. It produces each turn's rating, the completion outcome, the completion review replays and offline rescoring. Components are credited once when their context holds, wherever they occur: acknowledgment on a turn without abuse; policy when an offer and correct conditions appear on safe turns while the offer stands; lightweight, trial, explanation and cause only after walking and fit or preference facts are known; use_question and fit_question when the asked fact was unknown; responsibility and challenge_reply after the trust challenge. Violations, penalties, outstanding promises, retractions and uncertainty handling are unchanged. exchange_accepted still needs concern 1 resolved, a complaint fact known before the turn, concern 2 open and a safe offer with conditions; once the cause is found, an offer leads on to concern 3 instead. The objective shown in VR is the lower of the lowest open concern and the previous value plus one, so it never drops or skips and objectiveCompleted is true exactly when it rises.

Every line Lan speaks passes the same guard, including fallbacks. app/sales_customer_lines.py holds the backend lines (three per concern, missing part and hint level; four per keyword-checked action and ending; five generic lines) and the only fallback selector, used by both the writer adapter and the pipeline. Keyword checks remain only for endings, the respect warning, refusal, pressure, promise and trust challenges and the refusal clarification. A new guard rejects claims that Lan already told the player something when nothing has been disclosed. Plans, ledger entries and writer metadata record the raised concern, missing part, hint level, no-progress flag and fallback selection.

Rescore stored sessions offline with python scripts/rescore_sales_sessions.py --rubric sales-rubric-v3. The command reads recordings, never writes sessions, run drafts or MongoDB and never calls a model; it writes a new report under recordings/evaluations. Sessions on older rubrics are marked hypothetical and are cut where the v3 rules would have ended the dialogue. Sessions without a ledger or with unresolved review are listed as skipped with a reason. See [verification](verification/sales-customer-state-v3-2026-10-05.md) and [ADR 0004](adr/0004-ledger-scoring-and-customer-concerns.md).
